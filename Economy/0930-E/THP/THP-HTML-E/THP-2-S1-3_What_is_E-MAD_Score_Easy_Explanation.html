What is the E-MAD Score? ~ A New Measure for Corporate "Misconduct" ~

1. What is the E-MAD Score in a nutshell?

The E-MAD (Economically-Mutually Assured Destruction) Score is a new "measure" to quantify the "negative impact" that companies and organizations have had on society and the environment.

The higher the score, the higher the "misconduct level," meaning the greater the damage to society and the environment.

The purpose of this score is not just to punish companies that have done wrong. It is a tool to create common rules for society as a whole, saying "let's stop doing these things," and to aim for a future called "The Horizon Protocol (THP)" where everyone becomes more prosperous.

2. Why is such a score necessary?

There are countless companies that pollute the environment, treat employees unfairly, or break social rules. Such actions threaten our lives and the future of our planet.

However, objectively judging "how bad something is" is a very difficult problem. The E-MAD score visualizes the problem by showing the "magnitude of the negative impact" as a score from "0 to 100" that is easy for anyone to understand.

This allows us to objectively judge which companies are acting well and which companies are acting problematically, based on objective criteria.

3. How is the score determined? 3 Key Points

The E-MAD score is calculated by multiplying three main factors.

Point ①: Magnitude of Damage (Base Score)

First, the extent of the damage caused by the action is scored. This is the "base score" that forms the foundation of the score. The evaluation covers the following three aspects:

  • Impact on People (Human): How much harm was done to human life, health, and human rights?

  • Impact on the Environment (Environment): How much nature and ecosystems were destroyed?

  • Impact on Social Rules (Governance): How much social trust was undermined by lying, cheating, or other misconduct?

Point ②: Was it intentional? (Intent)

Next, the action is evaluated as to whether it was "intentional" or "accidental."

  • Done intentionally and systematically → The score will be higher.

  • It was an accidental mistake → The score will be lower.

Naturally, actions taken with malicious intent should be held to a higher degree of responsibility.

Point ③: Breadth of Impact (Scale)

Finally, the extent to which the impact of the action spread is evaluated.

  • Impact affects the entire planet → The score will be higher.

  • It was a problem limited to a very small area → The score will be lower.

For example, whether the wastewater from a factory polluted only the adjacent river or flowed into the ocean and affected fish worldwide makes a big difference in the seriousness of the problem.

4. Concrete Image

For example, it's like this:

  • Case A: Small accidental mistake

    • A restaurant mistakenly displayed incorrect allergy information. Fortunately, no health damage occurred.

    • Result → The E-MAD score will be low. This is because the damage was small, it was not intentional, and the scope of impact was limited.

  • Case B: Large-scale intentional pollution

    • A large company concealed the fact that it was discharging harmful chemicals into a river to cut costs. As a result, it caused widespread environmental pollution and health damage to many residents.

    • Result → The E-MAD score will be very high. This is because the damage was large, it was intentional, and the scope of impact was wide.

In this way, the E-MAD score is a powerful tool for evaluating various problems that occur in society based on fair and easy-to-understand criteria.
