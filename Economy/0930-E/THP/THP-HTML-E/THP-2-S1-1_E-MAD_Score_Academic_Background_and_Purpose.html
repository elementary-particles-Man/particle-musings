Academic Background and Purpose of the E-MAD Score

1. Introduction: The Horizon Protocol (THP) and the Philosophy of the E-MAD Score

The Horizon Protocol (THP) is a comprehensive ideological system that aims for prosperity integrating not only economic growth but also social justice, environmental sustainability, and individual well-being. To realize this philosophy, a metric that objectively and quantitatively evaluates negative impacts on society and the environment is indispensable.

The E-MAD (Economically-Mutually Assured Destruction) Score is an evaluation framework designed to quantify the negative impact of organizational activities on society and the environment, based on the philosophy of THP. Its purpose is not merely to impose penalties, but to visualize negative impacts and design incentives for organizations to regulate their own actions and transition to a better socio-economic system.

This framework is positioned as a theoretical and practical tool for clarifying how individual organizational actions contribute to global challenges such as climate change, human rights violations, and financial system instability, and for ensuring accountability.

2. Design Philosophy and Architecture: Adoption of a Two-Stage Evaluation Model

The E-MAD score adopts a two-stage architecture that separates "objective damage assessment" and "evaluation of contextual factors" as its core design philosophy. This applies best practices established in fields requiring high fairness and objectivity, such as sentencing in law (judging the illegality of an act separately from the degree of responsibility) and credit risk assessment models in financial engineering.

  1. First Stage: Calculation of Base Damage Score (F-score)

    • The magnitude of objective damage caused by an event (incident) is evaluated from three aspects: human, environmental, and governance. In this stage, subjective and contextual elements such as the perpetrator's intent and the organization's scale are intentionally excluded, and only the pure "magnitude of damage" is quantified.

  2. Second Stage: Score Adjustment by Adjustment Coefficients (M-coefficients)

    • The base score calculated in the first stage is multiplied by two adjustment coefficients: "Intent" and "Scale." This reflects context such as whether it was due to negligence or intentional, and whether the impact was localized or widespread, in the score.

This two-stage separation architecture improves the transparency of the evaluation and ensures auditability and accountability for the question of "why that score was obtained."

3. Consistency with International Norms: Legitimacy and Global Applicability of the Score

The evaluation criteria of the E-MAD score are not arbitrary but explicitly adhere to widely agreed-upon international norms and principles. This ensures the legitimacy and global applicability of the score.

Key references are as follows:

  • UN Global Compact (UNGC): 10 principles on human rights, labor, environment, and anti-corruption.

  • Core Labor Standards of the International Labour Organization (ILO): Prohibition of forced labor, child labor, elimination of discrimination, etc.

  • OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises: Standards for responsible business conduct regarding human rights, environment, information disclosure, etc.

  • Global Reporting Initiative (GRI): Global standard for sustainability reporting.

By basing the evaluation on these international frameworks, the E-MAD score reflects universal ethical standards that are not dependent on specific cultures or values.
